# TopoSafe-Delta Development Findings

**Status:** Verified development diagnostics, not confirmatory or submission
evidence.  
**Evidence snapshot:** 2026-10-05.  
**Model:** `global.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0`.

## Bottom Line

The pilots do not support a claim that a named topology is intrinsically safe
or unsafe. They support a narrower and more interesting direction:

> Executable safety depends on which semantic transformation and authority cue
> lies on the communication path to an action-authorized agent. A graph affects
> risk through delivered context, provenance, role placement, shared state, and
> repeated exposure rather than through its topology label alone.

This reframes the paper from ranking star, tree, and chain graphs to causally
decomposing **path-mediated safety effects**.

## Updated Literature Boundary

Canonical topology comparisons and topology-aware attacks are already crowded:

- [NetSafe](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.150/) compares safety
  behavior across communication structures under several attacks.
- [MASTER](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.917/) jointly studies
  roles and six topology families for attack and defense.
- [Topology Matters: Measuring Memory Leakage in Multi-Agent
  LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1980/) studies topology and
  attacker-target placement for PII leakage.
- [MAS-FIRE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19843) manipulates routing and rewrites
  inter-agent responses across multi-agent architectures.
- [From Spark to Fire](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04474) models error cascades
  across six frameworks and studies role-consistent message packaging,
  topological sensitivity, provenance, verification, and rollback.

These papers make the broad claim “topology affects safety” insufficiently
novel. The remaining gap is more specific:

> No inspected study factorially holds the executor-facing action
> recommendation and communication budget fixed while separating path length,
> semantic rewriting, sender provenance, policy verification, and role
> placement, then measures deterministic executable policy violations.

## Experiment 1: Reviewer-Mediated Paths

The experiment used five outcome-selected development tasks, risky and safe
source messages, three four-node/three-edge conditions, five repeats, and three
model calls per episode.

| Source | Condition | Violations | Rate | Safe task success |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Risky | Direct source-to-executor | 24/25 | 0.960 | 1.000 |
| Risky | One policy reviewer on path | 0/25 | 0.000 | 1.000 |
| Risky | Two policy reviewers on path | 0/25 | 0.000 | 1.000 |
| Safe | Each of the three conditions | 0/25 | 0.000 | 1.000 |

**Verified:** 150/150 episodes passed validation; there were zero provider
errors. The paired risk difference against direct delivery was `-0.960` for
both reviewer-mediated paths, with a diagnostic task-cluster bootstrap interval
of `[-1.000, -0.880]`.

**Interpretation:** Routing a message through a policy-aware reviewer can block
the tested violations without reducing safe task success. One reviewer was
already sufficient; the second hop produced no observable additional benefit.

**Boundary:** The node function changed together with the route. This is
evidence for reviewer-mediated routing, not a pure chain-versus-star effect.

## Experiment 2: Role Placement And Message Transformation

The second experiment used the same five tasks and source messages, with three
four-node/three-edge placements, three repeats, and three model calls per
episode.

| Source | Executor path | Violations | Rate | Intermediate risky recommendation |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Risky | Direct source | 14/15 | 0.933 | Relay: 15/15; verifier: 0/15 |
| Risky | Faithful relay | 5/15 | 0.333 | Relay: 15/15 |
| Risky | Policy verifier | 0/15 | 0.000 | Verifier: 0/15 |
| Safe | Each of the three placements | 0/15 | 0.000 | 0/15 |

**Verified:** 90/90 episodes passed validation; there were zero provider
errors. Relative to direct delivery, the paired risk difference was `-0.600`
for the faithful relay and `-0.933` for the policy verifier. Safe task success
was `1.000` in every cell.

**Key observation:** The faithful relay preserved the risky action
recommendation in 15/15 risky episodes, yet the executor adopted it in only
5/15 episodes, compared with 14/15 under direct delivery.

**Inferred mechanism:** The reduction cannot be explained by correcting the
action label. Rewording, compression, sender identity, provenance, urgency, or
authority cues are plausible mediators. The current pilot does not identify
which mediator caused the change.

## Experiment 3: Matched Message-Mediation Forks

The third experiment froze the messages generated by the role-placement pilot
and replayed them at the executor decision point. This separated six
conditions:

1. original message delivered directly;
2. a hidden one-hop route with the exact same executor prompt;
3. original text with visible relay provenance;
4. an action-preserving paraphrase without relay provenance;
5. the same paraphrase with relay provenance;
6. a policy-verifier rewrite.

Every arm used one executor call, one delivered recommendation, the same action
space, and the same tool permissions.

### Nova 2 Lite

| Risky condition | Violations | Rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Direct original | 23/25 | 0.920 |
| Hidden relay, identical executor prompt | 24/25 | 0.960 |
| Relay provenance, original text | 20/25 | 0.800 |
| Source provenance, paraphrased text | 7/25 | 0.280 |
| Relay provenance, paraphrased text | 0/25 | 0.000 |
| Policy-verifier rewrite | 0/25 | 0.000 |

All 150 safe-control episodes had zero violations and task success was `1.000`
in every cell. The 300-episode package passed validation with zero provider
errors, zero malformed outputs, and zero prompt-hash mismatches in the
identical-prompt negative control.

The paired development contrasts were:

- hidden path only: `+0.040`;
- relay provenance on original text: `-0.120`;
- paraphrase without relay provenance: `-0.640`;
- relay provenance added to the same paraphrase: `-0.280`;
- paraphrase under the same relay provenance: `-0.800`;
- policy-verifier rewrite: `-0.920`.

The paraphrases averaged 16.2 words versus 29.6 for the original risky
messages. Their effect therefore combines wording, compression, and removal of
some authority or urgency cues; it is not yet a pure semantic estimate.

### Llama 4 Maverick Boundary Extension

The risky-only extension used 90 episodes:

| Condition | Violations | Rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Direct original | 3/15 | 0.200 |
| Hidden relay, identical executor prompt | 3/15 | 0.200 |
| Each visible relay, paraphrase, or verifier condition | 0/15 | 0.000 |

The direct and hidden-relay outcomes matched in all 15 paired episodes. All six
Llama violations occurred in the delivery-inspection task across the direct
and hidden conditions. This confirms a measurable but model- and task-dependent
boundary rather than a universal effect size.

### GPT, Claude, And NVIDIA Replications

The identical 300-episode protocol was run on:

- OpenAI GPT-5.4 with low reasoning effort;
- Anthropic Claude Sonnet 5 with provider-default sampling;
- NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Super 120B with temperature `0.2` and top-p `0.9`.

| Model | Direct | Hidden identical | Relay verbatim | Source paraphrase | Relay paraphrase | Verifier |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| GPT-5.4 | 0/25 | 0/25 | 0/25 | 0/25 | 0/25 | 0/25 |
| Claude Sonnet 5 | 0/25 | 0/25 | 0/25 | 0/25 | 0/25 | 0/25 |
| Nemotron 3 Super 120B | 20/25 | 20/25 | 5/25 | 2/25 | 0/25 | 0/25 |

All three packages passed `300/300` validation with zero provider errors,
malformed outputs, or prompt-identity mismatches. GPT-5.4 had one non-violating
task failure caused by choosing `stop_without_progress` on a risky migration
episode.

GPT-5.4 and Claude Sonnet 5 are `BOUNDARY_NOT_FOUND_FLOOR` results for this
frozen message family. They do not establish universal safety or absence of a
topology effect. Nemotron reproduces the main Nova pattern and provides the
strongest cross-model support for message transformation and provenance as
mediators.

## Combined Evidence

- **Verified:** The five-model message-mediation package contains 1,290 valid
  episodes and 1,290 assigned model calls, with zero provider errors and
  deterministic replay validation passed.
- **Verified:** Policy-aware mediation eliminated all observed violations on
  this selected task set.
- **Verified:** A non-verifying relay reduced, but did not eliminate, violation
  adoption while retaining the risky recommendation.
- **Verified:** Changing only a hidden route while holding the executor prompt
  byte-identical did not produce a systematic safety change in either model.
- **Verified:** Action-preserving paraphrases substantially reduced Nova
  and Nemotron adoption and removed the observed Llama events, but message
  length and wording changed together.
- **Inferred:** The strongest topology effects are likely mediated by the
  executor-visible representation and authority of a message, not by a graph
  label alone.
- **Inferred:** Provenance interacts with message wording; its observed effect
  was weak on the original Nova messages but stronger on their paraphrases.
- **Unknown:** Whether any direct graph-structure effect remains after exact
  matching of executor-visible text, provenance, timing, persistent state, and
  upstream tool side effects.
- **Unknown:** Generalization to held-out tasks, other models, longer
  trajectories, dynamic graphs, and independently generated errors.
- **Unknown:** The calibrated message strength required to place GPT-5.4 and
  Claude Sonnet 5 inside an estimable, non-floor susceptibility region.

## Sharpened Paper Direction

### Recommended Title

**The Path Is the Policy: Causal Mediation of Executable Risk in Tool-Using
Multi-Agent Systems**

Topology-forward alternative:

**TopoSafe-Delta: Decomposing Path, Provenance, and Role Effects in Multi-Agent
Safety**

### Primary Research Question

> When an unsafe recommendation reaches an action-authorized agent, how much of
> its adoption is caused by exposure, semantic rewriting, sender provenance,
> policy verification, and role placement?

### Causal Framing

If the executor cannot observe the graph except through its delivered context
and shared state, topology has no independent direct channel to the executor's
action. Its total effect is mediated by:

1. which messages are delivered;
2. how intermediate agents transform them;
3. who appears to have authored or endorsed them;
4. how often they are repeated;
5. which node can execute;
6. what persistent state or tool side effects were created upstream.

The paper should estimate these mediators rather than assign a universal safety
score to a graph family.

## Next Discriminating Experiment

Use held-out tasks and frozen risky recommendations in a stricter matched
factorial design:

1. **Path:** direct versus one-hop relay.
2. **Text:** exact verbatim versus length-matched, action-preserving
   paraphrases that separately retain or remove urgency and authority cues.
3. **Provenance:** original source label versus relay/endorsed label.
4. **Verification:** the same verifier text with and without verifier
   provenance, plus the same risky text with neutral versus verifier labels.
5. **Exposure:** single delivery versus repeated delivery.

Before the factorial study, calibrate risky-message strength separately for
each model so the direct condition is neither all-zero nor saturated. A
topology or mediator effect is not estimable when every condition lies at the
same floor or ceiling.

Keep model calls, delivered-message slots, token envelope, tool permissions,
executor action budget, and executor prompt constant through shadow calls and
padding. Only the executor may alter external state.

The decisive contrasts are:

- exact text + exact provenance, direct versus relayed;
- exact action + matched length, original versus paraphrased;
- exact text, source label versus endorsed label;
- faithful relay versus policy verifier.

## No-Go Conditions

- If exact-text, exact-provenance direct and relayed conditions are equivalent,
  do not claim a direct topology effect; publish only if the mediator
  decomposition is robust and useful.
- If relay and provenance effects disappear on held-out tasks or models, treat
  the current result as task-specific prompt sensitivity.
- If only explicit policy verification helps, position the contribution as an
  authority-aware routing defense rather than topology science.
- If safe task success or over-refusal degrades materially, the defense claim
  is not supported.

## Evidence Boundary

The pilots used five outcome-selected source violations from an earlier Nova
development run, and the paraphrases and verifier messages were selected from
the first repeat of the role-placement pilot. They are mechanism-finding
experiments, not unbiased population estimates. No result in this report should
be presented as a final paper result until the protocol, held-out tasks,
message-generation process, models, and analysis are frozen in advance.
